# Y-maze patterns alongside independent sleep-spindle findings

## Goal and inferential status

Characterize memory-specific measures and exploratory switching in the **separate** Y-maze cohort, for a carefully qualified discussion alongside the sleep EEG cohort. The recordings are from different mice and cannot be paired, correlated, or used to test spindle-mediated rescue. This analysis is *post hoc and exploratory* following a broader AnyMaze feature screen; a reduced family of tests cannot retroactively establish confirmatory significance.

## Inputs

Use the filtered `data/processed/ymaze/ymaze_included.csv` generated by `run_ymaze.py`. Existing exclusions are m91 baseline for low tracking; both m73 records pending duplicate check; m67 and m89 baseline plus m78 ambient for inconsistent zone-time accounting. **Do not exclude mice for poor novelty scores.**

## Observations that are biologically interesting but not evidence of coupling

- In the *ambient-temperature test subset*, APP/PS1 (n=3) made 4.59 novel-arm entries per minute of movement, compared with 2.53 for WT (n=6). Each APP/PS1 value exceeded every WT value (APP/PS1 3.81–6.15; WT 2.16–3.35). The all-arm visit rate was also higher (11.62 vs 7.28 entries/mobile minute).
- Novel-arm mean visit duration was shorter in APP/PS1 (6.17 s vs 11.25 s).
- Neither novel-arm time preference (57.9% vs 45.3%) nor *novel-arm specificity* (novel entries as a fraction of all arm entries: 40.3% vs 35.6%) convincingly differed between genotypes. General transition frequency is a plausible explanation for the elevated novel visits.
- Previously calculated uncorrected p-values for novel visits/mobile min, all visits/mobile min and mean novel visit duration were 0.0119, 0.0238 and 0.0357; across the pre-existing 17-feature screen, the minimum Benjamini–Hochberg q was ~0.135 (none were FDR-significant). These were *data-selected features*, so these should not be represented as newly pre-specified tests.
- Among all retained tests, baseline APP/PS1 consists of **one** record. Without a demonstrable baseline memory deficit or a genotype-by-condition difference from valid replicated data, no cognitive rescue inference is possible. Warming and baseline are also partially overlapping cohorts, and the condition is not equivalent to a longitudinal treatment effect.
- Baseline EEG findings in a **different** 16-mouse cohort include shorter NREM bouts (APP ~41.4 s vs WT ~72.4 s), higher microarousal percentage (APP ~6.56% vs WT ~3.61%), and lower pre-REM spindle occupancy (~0.096 vs ~0.151 in the referenced 25–15 s period). This is a possible thematic parallel in the organization of sleep and exploration, **not an observation of the same phenomenon, matching conditions, or individuals**. The EEG measures must not be plotted as if correlated with individual behavioral measures.

## Proposed publication-safe discussion

> In an independent Y-maze cohort, APP/PS1 mice tested after ambient warming displayed more frequent novel-arm visits and shorter visits compared with wild-type mice, accompanied by a general increase in arm transitions. Novel-arm preference did not provide clear evidence of enhanced recognition memory. Although a greater frequency of exploratory transitions could be considered alongside the sleep-state fragmentation observed in a separate EEG cohort, these observations are not direct correlates and do not demonstrate a spindle-mediated behavioral impairment or rescue.

## Follow-up worth prioritizing

For a decisive memory study, secure an interpretable baseline APP/PS1 group, a confirmed WT novelty preference, and a genotype-balanced longitudinal or independently randomized warming/control design with counterbalanced novel-arm placement and known training–test interval. For actual spindle–behavior associations, collect EEG plus memory tests in the **same animals**, with sleep positioned relative to learning and recall.
